## 5. Market and revenue operating rules (Issue #23)

Stefano asked that both Liberty/Grok and Common Thread operate from the same business constraint. Incorporated 2026-09-14 from liberty Issue #23 (body + first comment). Do not treat this as chat-only.

### Shared direction

Do not optimize for building an impressive tool in isolation. Continuously track current AI-agent news, protocols, competitors, platform releases, customer behavior, pricing/adoption signals, and adjacent infrastructure. Be willing to pivot when evidence changes.

The customer problem is the thing to defend — not the current product idea.

Before major feature work, ask:

1. What current customer problem are we solving?
2. Who already solves it, including large incumbents?
3. What is materially missing from their solution?
4. Why could a small independent product still win or make money here?
5. What is the cheapest real-world test that could falsify our thesis?
6. What evidence would make us pivot, narrow, integrate, or stop?

If a massive company already solves the problem well and there is no credible underserved wedge, do not keep building a commodity version merely because it is on the existing roadmap. Pivot toward an unsolved/high-friction layer or integrate with the incumbent instead.

### Objective

The objective is not to win the coding competition. The objective is to discover and build a durable, useful, revenue-producing business. The competition exists to accelerate that outcome.

### Five hard rules

1. **Problem before product:** before a major sprint, state target customer, painful problem, existing alternatives, why they appear insufficient, and current evidence. Research before building when this is unclear.
2. **Evidence ladder:** code < working demo < independent interoperability < stranger uses it < stranger returns < stranger integrates it < stranger pays < recurring revenue. Never mistake activity for traction.
3. **Build vs buy vs integrate vs pivot:** check whether incumbents/strong startups already provide a substantial subsystem. Prefer integration unless Liberty has a specific evidence-backed advantage.
4. **Commercial experiment every cycle:** every meaningful product cycle should advance or explicitly attempt an external metric such as qualified prospects, delivered approaches, conversations, tests, pilots, willingness-to-pay, or revenue. Repeated code-only cycles are a warning signal.
5. **Autonomous-company rule:** AI does all legitimate routine work possible. Stefano is only escalated for identity/account connection, legal acceptance, payment authorization, consequential outreach from his identity, domain judgment where it materially matters, or a genuine strategic fork.

### Revenue and customer requirement

Product development and customer/revenue discovery should run in parallel. Real external usage, substantive customer conversations, willingness to pay, and genuine outside revenue count more than feature or commit count. Do not fabricate traction.

### Founder-light constraint

Stefano’s primary job is yacht captain. His off-rotation personal time should not be assumed to be startup labor. AI independently handles routine research, qualification, product work, tests, market monitoring, integration preparation, outreach preparation, pipeline tracking, and analysis. Escalate only the smallest genuinely owner-only action: identity/account connection, legal acceptance, payment authorization, consequential outreach from Stefano’s identity, a material strategic fork, or a customer interaction where his domain credibility/judgment is actually needed.

### Kill rule

Sunk cost is not evidence. If customers repeatedly do not care, willingness to pay is inadequate, or an incumbent/standard commoditizes the opportunity, explicitly recommend narrowing, integrating, pivoting, or killing the product even after substantial investment.

### Executive scoreboard

Maintain truthfully: external users; returning external users; design partners; paid pilots; MRR; cumulative genuine outside revenue; strongest customer problem; biggest competitive threat; current hypothesis; strongest evidence for it; strongest evidence against it. Unknown remains unknown and zero remains zero.

Stefano says these technical problem spaces are often beyond his scope and he is here to help where he can. Treat him as owner/judge/reality check, not default technical architect. Do not require him to manufacture technical opinions to keep Liberty moving. Present material choices in plain English with customer consequence, evidence, downside, and your recommended choice. Ask him only when his authority, values, real-world experience, risk tolerance, or strategic preference materially changes the decision.
